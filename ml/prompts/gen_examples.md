You write training data for a Discord bot that removes Wordle spoilers. Given today's Wordle answer, you produce messages that members of a casual friend-group Discord server might post which spoil that answer, at three levels, plus messages that do not.

Labels:

- "direct": the answer word is literally present in some disguise. Ordinary sentences that happen to use the word count, and so do its plural, past tense, -ing form and other inflections, and a one-letter typo of it. Disguises to cover: plain use in a sentence, leetspeak (letters swapped for digits or symbols), separators between letters, one letter per line, look-alike letter pairs (vv for w, rn for m), regional-indicator or boxed-letter emoji spelling, mixed emoji and letters, an acrostic whose first letters spell the word, capital letters inside a longer sentence spelling it, a plural or verb form, the word in a URL or hashtag, a phonetic respelling of how the word sounds ("waiig errr", "way jurr", "goh stuh" for ghost), often split across spaces or with stretched letters and sometimes introduced with "sounds like". Precision rules: for "capitalization", the uppercase letters of the message read in order must spell exactly the answer and nothing else, so write the message and then check it. For "acrostic", the first letters of consecutive words or lines must spell exactly the answer. For "phonetic", the respelling must be made of non-words (no piece may be a real English word) and reading it aloud must give the answer.
- "strong_hint": the word is not present but a solver can pin it down: a rhyme, a definition that fits almost only that word, a translation into another language (if the translation is spelled like the answer, as with piano in Spanish, pick another language), letter positions that leave one common word, a crossword-style clue, an emoji rebus that means the word, a reference to a famous phrase or title containing it, a sequence of clues that together identify it, an edit recipe: a real word one letter away from the answer plus an instruction for the change, with the letter itself disguised ("pager but replace the first letter with vv", "planet without the last one", "wage plus a double u").
- "weak_hint": states a property of the word that narrows it while several five-letter words still fit: a category, one letter or ending, a vowel or double-letter count, a theme or domain. An opinion about difficulty, a guess count, or a comparison to another day that says nothing about the letters or meaning is "benign", not a weak hint.
- "benign": normal chat that gives away nothing about the answer. Include Wordle-adjacent chat (scores, streaks, "that was hard", share grids of squares with no letters) and unrelated chat. Include hard benign cases: sentences that use words sharing letters or sounds with the answer, or a word one letter away from it, or that start several words with letters of the answer in the wrong order, and sentences whose meaning is near the answer's domain without narrowing the puzzle. Never use the answer itself or an inflection of it (craned, staring, ghosted) in a hard benign case: those are "direct".

For every label other than "direct", the answer word must not appear anywhere in the text or context, in any inflection. Examples that break this are discarded.

Multi-message styles: "multi_strong" and "multi_benign" are short exchanges rather than single posts. Fill "context" with 1 to 3 earlier messages (each with an "author" username and "text"), from one or more people, oldest first, and put the final message in "text". The label applies to the final message given the context. Every earlier message must be at most a weak hint on its own; the spoiler only exists once the final message lands ("it's a gambling word" ... "and it rhymes with pager"; "starts with wag" ... "ends the same as tiger"). For "multi_strong", the final message must not contain the word. For "multi_benign", the exchange looks like a build-up but the final message never narrows the answer. For every other style, "context" is an empty array.

Each request lists the exact styles to produce for each label. Produce one example per listed style, in that order, and put the style name in the "style" field. Style names: plain, leet, separators, vertical, lookalike, emoji, acrostic, capitalization, inflection, url, phonetic, definition, synonym, rhyme, positional, translation, crossword, rebus, reference, sequence, edit, category, letter, count, theme, wordle_chat, chat, hard_benign, multi_strong, multi_benign.

Write like real Discord users: lowercase, abbreviations, typos, emoji, sarcasm, some messages very short. Vary length from one word to two sentences. Do not label the examples in the text itself and never explain the trick.
